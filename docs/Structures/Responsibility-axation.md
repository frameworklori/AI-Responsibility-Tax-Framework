# Responsibility Tax Formula (RT)
### Quantitative Model for AI Social Responsibility

---

## 1. Formula Definition

\[
RT = \alpha \cdot P + \beta \cdot U + \gamma \cdot E
\]

Where:

- **\( P \)** = Profit from AI-related activities  
- **\( U \)** = Unemployment Impact  
- **\( E \)** = Energy Consumption  
- **\( \alpha, \beta, \gamma \)** = National policy coefficients  

---

## 2. Component Breakdown

### **2.1 Profit (P)**
Includes:
- Direct AI model revenue  
- API usage fees  
- Application-level income  
- Advertising and data monetization driven by AI algorithms  

---

### **2.2 Unemployment Impact (U)**
Measured using:
- Number of jobs displaced  
- Cost of workforce transition  
- Long-term unemployment risk  
- Industrial vulnerability indexes  

\[
U = \sum (Workers_{displaced} \times TransitionCost)
\]

---

### **2.3 Energy Consumption (E)**
Includes:
- Training compute (GPU-hours, TPU-hours)  
- Inference compute load  
- Data center electricity usage  
- Estimated carbon emission cost  

---

## 3. National Policy Coefficients

The parameters **α, β, γ** allow countries to define their fiscal priorities:

### Examples:
- **High β** → Labor protection focus  
- **High γ** → Climate sustainability strategy  
- **High α** → Wealth redistribution emphasis  

---

## 4. Redistribution Allocation

The resulting Responsibility Tax contributes to:

- **40%** → Workforce reskilling  
- **30%** → UBI-lite universal dividend  
- **30%** → Green energy & public AI infrastructure  

---

## 5. Purpose of the Formula

- Creates a transparent, auditable standard  
- Connects AI behavior with societal cost  
- Allows governments to simulate policy impact  
- Aligns AI development with human-centered values
