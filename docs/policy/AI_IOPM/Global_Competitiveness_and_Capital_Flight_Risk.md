# Global Competitiveness and Capital Flight Risk

If AI participation systems become too restrictive or costly in one country, capital and infrastructure investment may relocate to more permissive jurisdictions.

## Current Reality Signal

- AI infrastructure competition between the U.S., China, Gulf states, and Europe is intensifying.
- Hyperscalers are deploying capital globally.
- Energy access and regulatory flexibility increasingly influence AI investment location.

## AIRTF Structural Mapping

- Compute concentration: capital may cluster where compute deployment is cheapest and fastest.
- Sovereign competition: states may compete through subsidies, energy policy, permitting, and tax treatment.
- Infrastructure dependency: countries that fail to retain AI infrastructure may become downstream renters.
- Strategic economic positioning: participation systems must preserve democratic legitimacy without undermining investment capacity.

## Possible Development Path

- A. Competitive democratic participation model.
- B. AI capital flight.
- C. Regulatory arbitrage.
- D. Sovereign subsidy competition.
- E. Fragmented AI infrastructure blocs.

## Indicators to Watch

- AI infrastructure relocation
- compute-capex geography
- sovereign subsidy programs
- energy-price arbitrage
- international tax competition

## What Would Falsify This Risk

- participation systems coexist with strong investment growth
- democratic infrastructure models remain globally competitive
- capital flight remains limited

## Open Questions

Can democratic ownership participation coexist with global AI infrastructure competition?
