# Generational Access and Entry Risk

AI-IOPM cannot stabilize long-term participation if ownership remains structurally inherited and inaccessible to younger generations.

## Current Reality Signal

- Homeownership access for younger households has weakened.
- Asset ownership is increasingly concentrated by age and wealth.
- AI-era infrastructure ownership may follow existing capital concentration patterns.
- Younger workers face entry-level labor pressure and lower wealth accumulation rates.

## AIRTF Structural Mapping

- Middle-class formation: ownership access affects household formation, wealth accumulation, and long-term participation.
- Ownership concentration: AI infrastructure shares may reinforce existing wealth patterns if entry mechanisms are absent.
- Intergenerational inequality: older and wealthier households may capture more infrastructure upside.
- Demand formation fragility: younger households without assets, wages, or ownership stakes may delay consumption, family formation, and entrepreneurship.

## Possible Development Path

- A. Citizen infrastructure share allocation.
- B. Birthright participation accounts.
- C. Sovereign participation pools.
- D. Inherited concentration deepens.
- E. AI ownership becomes dynastic capital.

## Indicators to Watch

- age-based asset ownership
- first-time investor participation
- youth wealth accumulation
- generational wealth gaps
- citizen participation account growth

## What Would Falsify This Risk

- younger households gain meaningful ownership access
- infrastructure participation broadens across generations
- intergenerational concentration stabilizes

## Open Questions

Can AI-era ownership participation remain democratic without structural entry mechanisms for younger generations?
