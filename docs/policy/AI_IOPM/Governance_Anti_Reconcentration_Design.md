# Governance Anti-Reconcentration Design

Formal ownership distribution alone does not guarantee real democratic participation if voting and governance reconcentrate in asset-management intermediaries.

## Current Reality Signal

- Modern finance already concentrates voting influence through large asset managers.
- Index-fund concentration creates indirect governance centralization.
- Citizen ownership can become symbolic if governance remains centralized.

## AIRTF Structural Mapping

- Access infrastructure concentration: governance over AI infrastructure can become concentrated even when nominal ownership is dispersed.
- Financial intermediary capture: asset managers, custodians, and proxy systems may exercise practical control.
- Symbolic ownership risk: citizens may hold economic claims without meaningful governance participation.

## Governance Safeguards

- voting caps
- anti-cross-holding rules
- citizen governance seats
- proxy-vote transparency
- mandatory diversification rules
- fiduciary separation obligations
- anti-monopoly thresholds
- public audit systems
- decentralized reporting systems

## Indicators to Watch

- asset-manager voting concentration
- proxy-voting centralization
- citizen governance participation rates
- ownership dispersion metrics

## What Would Falsify This Risk

- governance remains broadly distributed
- voting concentration stays limited
- citizen oversight remains meaningful

## Open Questions

Can modern large-scale infrastructure ownership remain decentralized without recreating intermediary oligarchies?
