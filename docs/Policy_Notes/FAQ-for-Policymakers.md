# FAQ for Policymakers  
### AI-Responsibility-Tax Framework (AIRTF)

---

## **Q1. Is AIRTF a new tax, or a modification of existing taxes?**
AIRTF is a **hybrid** system:  
It introduces *new tax bases* (Compute, Robot, Sovereign Share) while integrating with existing corporate and capital tax structures.

---

## **Q2. Will this discourage AI innovation?**
No.  
AIRTF intentionally preserves incentives for:
- Long-term investment  
- Open-source contribution  
- High-efficiency compute  
- Environmentally responsible AI  

Speculation and irresponsible deployment → discouraged.  
Innovation → protected.

---

## **Q3. How do we measure AI-driven unemployment?**
Through the **Unemployment Impact Index (UII)**:
- Worker displacement count  
- Required transition cost  
- Sectoral vulnerability scoring  
- Longitudinal labor analytics  

---

## **Q4. How do we prevent companies from offshoring compute to avoid taxes?**
AIRTF supports:
- International compute registries  
- Compute location reporting laws  
- Cloud provider compliance frameworks  
- Border-adjusted compute taxes  

---

## **Q5. What prevents inequality from widening?**
The AI Social Dividend Fund:
- 30% → UBI-lite  
- 40% → workforce transition  
- 30% → green & public AI infrastructure  

This ensures broad social benefit.

---

## **Q6. Will AIRTF conflict with existing corporate tax laws?**
No.  
AIRTF is modular and can be layered atop existing fiscal structures.

---

## **Q7. Which countries should adopt AIRTF first?**
Ideal early adopters:
- AI-intensive economies  
- Countries with aging populations  
- Highly automated manufacturing regions  
- Digital-first city-states  

---

## **Q8. Can AIRTF be adopted without full five-pillar implementation?**
Yes.  
Countries may begin with:
- Responsibility Tax  
- Compute Tax  
- Robot Tax  
Then gradually integrate Sovereign Share & Public AI Works.
