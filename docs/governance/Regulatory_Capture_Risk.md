# Regulatory Capture Risk

Regulatory capture occurs when AI infrastructure owners shape rules to preserve their own chokepoints.

Risks include:

- lobbying dominance
- technical opacity
- procurement dependency
- standards capture
- revolving-door employment
- public reliance on private AI expertise

Public AI infrastructure and independent technical capacity are anti-capture tools.

## Capture of Access Infrastructure

AI-era capture may not only happen through lobbying.

It may happen through control of visibility, ranking, identity, payment, credit, and recommendation systems.

This creates soft economic control without formal ownership of all production. Firms or states may shape what people can find, buy, build, publish, finance, or become by controlling the infrastructure through which access is granted.

AIRTF therefore treats access infrastructure as a capture surface. Search systems, recommendation engines, app stores, AI assistants, digital identity, payment rails, credit scoring, compute access, and platform visibility controls require transparency, contestability, interoperability, and public-interest oversight.
