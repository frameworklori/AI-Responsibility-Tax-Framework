# Government Deficit as Liquidity Buffer

Government spending and deficits can act as a liquidity buffer when private-sector income weakens. AIRTF treats this as a stabilizing tool, but also as a warning signal when public borrowing becomes a substitute for broad wage circulation.

AI does not automatically destroy the economy. The danger is that productivity gains may concentrate faster than purchasing power can circulate.

## Deficits as Cash-Flow Injection

Government deficits inject cash flow into the economy through transfers, public wages, procurement, tax credits, subsidies, grants, defense spending, healthcare payments, interest payments, and infrastructure investment.

This can temporarily support household and corporate demand when private-sector income is under pressure. It can prevent a sharper demand collapse and keep businesses, households, and state systems liquid.

## Temporary Support and Long-Term Dependence

The risk is not that deficits are always bad. The risk is long-term fiscal dependence caused by a private economy that no longer circulates enough purchasing power through wages, broad ownership, or local business income.

If AI productivity gains concentrate upstream while household liquidity weakens downstream, deficits may become the recurring bridge between high productive capacity and insufficient consumer demand.

## Political Instability

Political instability can rise when deficits expand but broad purchasing power remains weak. Voters may see public debt increasing without feeling more secure in wages, housing, healthcare, food, education, or retirement.

That gap can produce distrust: the state appears to spend more, but ordinary liquidity remains strained.
