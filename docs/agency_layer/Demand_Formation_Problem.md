# Demand Formation Problem

AI can expand supply, automate production, and lower the marginal cost of many goods and services.

That does not mean demand automatically remains healthy, plural, or human-directed.

The production problem may be solved by AI, but the demand formation problem remains unresolved.

Demand is not only purchasing power. It is also preference discovery. People learn what they want by choosing, exploring, comparing, failing, revising, and changing their minds. A living economy needs those processes because future demand is not fully contained in past behavior.

AI-managed allocation can predict prior preferences with increasing precision. But prediction can become narrowing if systems mainly infer what users are likely to accept from historical signals. A society optimized around past behavior may become efficient at serving yesterday's preferences while weakening the formation of new ones.

This matters especially in a post-wage economy. If wages no longer serve as the primary demand signal, AIRTF must still explain:

- who decides what is produced
- who receives access
- how new preferences become visible
- how minority or emerging demand is discovered
- how people exit imposed allocation systems
- what signal replaces wage-backed market demand

AI may solve the production problem, but it does not automatically solve the demand formation problem.
