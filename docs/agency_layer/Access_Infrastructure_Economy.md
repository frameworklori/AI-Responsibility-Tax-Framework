# Access Infrastructure Economy

The key AI-era concentration risk is not only ownership of production. It is also ownership of access infrastructure.

Access infrastructure includes:

- recommendation systems
- search and discovery systems
- app stores
- AI assistants
- payment rails
- credit scoring systems
- cloud and compute access
- digital identity systems
- platform visibility controls

These systems determine what people can see, choose, buy, learn, build, and become.

Formal market choice may still exist while real access is shaped by hidden infrastructure control. A person may technically be free to buy, publish, build, or participate, while ranking systems, identity gates, payment systems, assistant defaults, app-store rules, and compute access determine whether that choice is practically reachable.

The 20th-century debate focused on ownership of production. The AI-era debate must also focus on ownership of access.

AIRTF therefore treats access infrastructure as a core economic layer. Ownership and governance of this layer can shape demand, opportunity, visibility, competition, and political power even when production itself becomes abundant.
