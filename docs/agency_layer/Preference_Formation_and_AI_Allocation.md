# Preference Formation and AI Allocation

Preferences are not stable inputs that AI can simply measure.

People often form preferences through the act of choosing. They discover taste, values, needs, ambitions, and limits through trial, comparison, social exposure, education, work, conflict, and surprise. Preference is not only a data point. It is a developmental process.

AI prediction can become preference calcification.

If AI systems allocate information, services, opportunities, credit, work, media, and social exposure based mainly on prior behavior, they may optimize for yesterday's self rather than tomorrow's possible self. The system may become better at predicting acceptance than at supporting growth.

This creates risks of:

- behavioral narrowing
- reduced discovery
- algorithmic dependency
- manipulation of desire
- weakened human agency

The concern is not anti-AI. AI can help people discover, compare, learn, and participate when designed around agency. The risk is AI allocation without access rights, transparency, contestability, or genuine exit.

An agency-preserving system should help people expand their possible selves, not quietly compress them into the most predictable version of past behavior.
