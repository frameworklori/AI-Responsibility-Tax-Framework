# Infrastructure Feudalism and Agency Risk

AIRTF already addresses infrastructure concentration, compute feudalism, integration-layer monopoly, and platform rent extraction.

The agency layer extends this analysis from economic rents to agency control.

Platform owners may not only extract value. They may shape what users perceive as possible. Search rankings, assistant defaults, recommendation systems, app-store access, identity systems, payment rails, and cloud access can define the practical boundaries of economic participation.

The risk is not only poverty or unemployment.

The risk is a society where humans become optimized consumers rather than self-directed economic agents.

In that society, people may receive goods, entertainment, services, and even subsidies while losing the ability to meaningfully shape demand, build alternatives, exit platforms, contest visibility, or participate in value creation.

This is infrastructure feudalism at the agency layer: dependence on privately or state-controlled gateways that mediate opportunity, preference, and participation.

AIRTF opposes this not because it opposes AI, but because it supports human agency, access sovereignty, demand discovery, and plural economic participation.
