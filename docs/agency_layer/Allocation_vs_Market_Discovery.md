# Allocation vs Market Discovery

Market systems are imperfect, unequal, and often distorted by monopoly, debt, advertising, inherited wealth, and political power.

Even so, markets provide one important function: decentralized preference discovery. They allow many people to reveal, revise, and contest demand through repeated choices, entry, exit, comparison, and failure.

Central allocation systems historically struggled because they lacked reliable feedback and exit mechanisms. Planners could not easily know changing local needs, unstable preferences, hidden constraints, or the value of alternatives that had not yet been tried.

AI improves computation. It does not automatically solve:

- unstable preferences
- strategic misrepresentation
- political capture
- lack of exit options
- corrupted feedback signals

AI allocation may become more efficient than past planning systems. It may also become more capable of behavioral control.

The central question is not whether AI can allocate better than older bureaucracies. The question is whether allocation systems preserve feedback, contestability, plural discovery, and meaningful exit.

AIRTF therefore distinguishes efficiency from agency. A system can allocate efficiently while narrowing human demand formation. A healthy AI-era economy needs both abundance and decentralized discovery.
