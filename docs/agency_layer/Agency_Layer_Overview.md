# Agency Layer Overview

AIRTF began as a civilization liquidity framework. This agency layer extends it into a framework for preserving human economic agency under AI-mediated abundance.

## Why This Layer Was Added

The original AIRTF thesis focused on whether AI productivity gains circulate through wages, taxes, purchasing power, and public investment.

The AIRTF x Claude dialogue revealed a deeper problem: even if AI lowers costs, even if subsidies preserve survival, and even if AI dividends stabilize consumption, humans may still lose economic agency if access, discovery, preference formation, and participation are controlled by concentrated infrastructure.

## How It Extends AIRTF

This layer does not replace the AIRTF thesis. It adds a second-layer question:

Can humans remain genuine economic agents when AI systems increasingly determine access, visibility, preference formation, and participation?

AI may solve the production problem, but it does not automatically solve the demand formation problem.

The agency layer adds attention to:

- human preference formation
- decentralized demand discovery
- access rights
- participation in value creation
- ownership or governance of AI infrastructure
- protection against platform or state-controlled allocation

## Relationship to Civilization Liquidity Risk

Civilization Liquidity Risk asks whether purchasing power keeps circulating broadly enough to sustain demand and stability.

The agency layer asks whether humans retain the ability to shape demand, not only fund it.

A society can have liquidity without freedom of access. It can have consumption without participation. It can have AI abundance while human demand formation becomes managed by opaque infrastructure.

## Relationship to Sovereign AI Equity

Sovereign AI Equity helps ensure that public systems and citizens share in the gains of AI infrastructure.

The agency layer expands this idea from revenue sharing to infrastructure governance. Public or shared claims on AI value should also support access rights, contestable discovery systems, transparent allocation, and participation in the institutions that mediate economic opportunity.

## Why Tax Policy Alone Is Insufficient

Tax policy can redistribute gains and stabilize purchasing power. It cannot by itself guarantee:

- fair discovery
- open access
- preference pluralism
- exit from dominant platforms
- accountable AI assistants
- transparent ranking and recommendation systems
- participation in value creation

Taxation can fund agency-preserving infrastructure, but it cannot substitute for access governance.

## Why Access Governance Must Be Added

Access infrastructure determines who can be seen, who can build, who can transact, who can learn, and which futures become imaginable.

AIRTF governance must therefore include:

- interoperability
- search and recommendation transparency
- payment and identity access rights
- compute access rules
- AI assistant accountability
- platform visibility contestability
- public and open protocol alternatives

The goal is pro-human agency, pro-access sovereignty, pro-demand discovery, anti-infrastructure feudalism, anti-platform dependency, and anti-state or corporate allocation monopoly.

## Observable Access Channels

Agency concerns must be tied to real-world infrastructure:

- AI assistants
- search
- recommendation systems
- cloud/compute access
- payment rails
- digital identity
- credit scoring
- app-store access

This layer should not expand into pure philosophy. It should remain tied to observable access channels, measurable concentration, contestability, interoperability, transparency, and the practical ability of people and firms to participate without dependency.
