# Reality Signal Map

AIRTF is not a prophecy. It is a civilization transition diagnostic framework.

This map connects current U.S. economic signals to AIRTF risk channels. Current evidence does not prove civilization liquidity collapse. It shows partial real-world overlap with AIRTF risk channels.

## Current Reality Signal

- AI adoption is still early and uneven across sectors.
- Layoffs and white-collar compression exist, but overall labor market collapse is not proven.
- U.S. consumer spending remains central to the economy; personal consumption expenditures were about 68.1% of GDP in Q1 2026.
- The U.S. labor market is still resilient overall, with April 2026 payrolls up 115,000 and unemployment at 4.3%, but white-collar and information-sector pressure remains a key signal to watch.
- Stock markets and AI valuations remain strong.
- U.S. household debt reached around $18.8 trillion in Q4 2025, with credit card and auto delinquencies elevated relative to the past decade.
- U.S. housing remains affordability-constrained: April 2026 existing home sales were weak, median price reached about $417,700, and first-time buyers were only about 33% of purchases.
- Rental vacancy in Q1 2026 was about 7.3%, showing that rent markets may be softening in some areas, but affordability stress can still persist.
- Government deficits and tax policy may be acting as liquidity buffers.

## AIRTF Structural Mapping

- Civilization Liquidity Risk: consumer spending remains structurally important, so household liquidity and wage circulation remain central diagnostic variables.
- White-collar compression: layoffs, entry-level hiring weakness, and information-sector pressure may indicate early AI-linked transition stress.
- Asset-market vs household-liquidity divergence: strong AI valuations can coexist with household debt stress and affordability constraints.
- Demand fragility: consumption may persist through credit, wealth effects, or fiscal support even if wage-based circulation weakens.
- Ownership concentration: AI infrastructure returns may concentrate in cloud, compute, model, platform, and data owners.
- Access infrastructure concentration: search, recommendation, AI assistants, payment rails, identity systems, app stores, and compute access can shape participation before formal market choice occurs.

## Possible Development Path

- Adaptation path: AI creates new industries, productivity gains diffuse, and labor markets adjust without major circulation damage.
- Concentration path: AI gains accrue mainly to infrastructure owners while wage growth and household liquidity weaken.
- Subsidized survival path: transfers, tax credits, public services, and deficits maintain consumption without restoring full participation.
- Access-feudalism path: platform or state access layers shape discovery, identity, payment, credit, compute, and opportunity.
- Hybrid reform path: tax tools, sovereign AI equity, labor transition, wage-floor reform, public AI infrastructure, and access governance reduce transition stress.

## Indicators to Watch

- labor share
- real wage growth
- white-collar hiring
- entry-level hiring
- household debt service
- credit card delinquency
- first-time buyer share
- rental burden
- AI infrastructure concentration
- deficit dependency
- consumer spending by income group

## What Would Falsify This Risk

- broad-based real wage growth
- AI creates new middle-class jobs faster than displacement
- household debt stress declines
- first-time homebuyer share recovers
- AI lowers core living costs without increasing dependency
- AI ownership and infrastructure access become broadly distributed

## Open Questions

- Which indicators best separate short-term adjustment from structural transition stress?
- How quickly can new AI-enabled occupations absorb displaced white-collar workers?
- Can access infrastructure become measurable enough for public diagnostics?
