# Future Pathway Matrix

AIRTF compares multiple pathways instead of treating any outcome as inevitable.

## 1. Adaptation Scenario

AI creates new industries and new participation pathways faster than displacement harms circulation.

### Core Mechanism

AI augments workers, lowers business formation costs, creates new occupations, and diffuses productivity gains broadly enough to preserve household liquidity.

### Current Signals Supporting It

- Overall labor market collapse is not proven.
- Healthcare, logistics, retail, services, and physical sectors still absorb workers.
- AI may reduce costs and expand new business models.

### Early Warning Indicators

- AI-native job creation accelerates.
- Entry-level hiring recovers.
- Real wages rise across income groups.
- New small businesses use AI without platform dependency.

### Falsification Signals

- Persistent white-collar unemployment.
- Labor share declines despite productivity growth.
- New AI jobs remain narrow, low-quality, or concentrated.

### Policy Implications

Support training, portability, open tools, entrepreneurship, public AI infrastructure, and fast transition channels.

### Open Questions

- Which AI-native sectors can support durable middle-class careers?

## 2. Concentration Scenario

AI productivity gains concentrate among infrastructure owners while wages and demand weaken.

### Core Mechanism

Compute, cloud, model, data, distribution, and platform owners capture productivity gains faster than wages and household income can adjust.

### Current Signals Supporting It

- AI and mega-cap tech valuations remain strong.
- Asset-market strength can diverge from household liquidity.
- AI infrastructure has high capital requirements.

### Early Warning Indicators

- S&P 500 concentration rises.
- Labor share declines.
- Platform rents and compute concentration rise.
- Wage growth lags productivity growth.

### Falsification Signals

- AI ownership and access become broadly distributed.
- Wage growth and labor share improve.
- Platform dependency declines.

### Policy Implications

Responsibility Tax, Compute Levy, Sovereign AI Equity, antitrust, interoperability, and public AI infrastructure become more relevant.

### Open Questions

- How much concentration is infrastructure necessity, and how much is avoidable market design?

## 3. Deflationary Relief Scenario

AI lowers major living costs enough to preserve real purchasing power despite wage stagnation.

### Core Mechanism

AI-driven productivity reduces prices in essentials such as healthcare administration, education, transportation, food logistics, housing production, and public services.

### Current Signals Supporting It

- AI can lower some production and coordination costs.
- Firms and governments are investing heavily in AI capacity.

### Early Warning Indicators

- Core household expenses fall in real terms.
- Real purchasing power improves without credit expansion.
- Consumer debt stress declines.

### Falsification Signals

- AI savings remain in corporate margins.
- Essential costs remain high.
- Debt and affordability stress persist.

### Policy Implications

Tax policy may need recalibration rather than expansion if AI gains broadly lower living costs without increasing dependency.

### Open Questions

- Which essential sectors are most likely to pass AI savings to households?

## 4. Subsidized Survival Scenario

Governments use UBI, tax credits, transfers, or public AI services to prevent collapse but do not restore agency.

### Core Mechanism

Fiscal policy sustains minimum consumption while labor-market participation, ownership access, and demand formation weaken.

### Current Signals Supporting It

- Government deficits and tax policy may already act as liquidity buffers.
- Household debt and affordability stress remain important.

### Early Warning Indicators

- Transfer payment dependency rises.
- Deficit dependency grows.
- Labor force participation weakens.
- Consumption beyond essentials stagnates.

### Falsification Signals

- Transfers bridge workers into productive roles.
- Entrepreneurship and household formation improve.
- Dependency declines over time.

### Policy Implications

Pair support with ownership rights, transition infrastructure, public AI services, and participation pathways.

### Open Questions

- What makes income support a bridge rather than a permanent stagnation channel?

## 5. Access Feudalism Scenario

Platforms or states control discovery, recommendation, identity, payment, credit, compute, and AI assistant layers.

### Core Mechanism

Access infrastructure determines practical participation even when formal market choice remains.

### Current Signals Supporting It

- AI assistants, search, recommendation, app stores, cloud, payment, identity, and credit systems increasingly mediate economic life.
- Users and firms often depend on platform visibility.

### Early Warning Indicators

- Assistant defaults concentrate.
- Search and recommendation audits remain weak.
- Compute access concentrates.
- SMEs become more dependent on platform visibility.

### Falsification Signals

- Interoperability improves.
- Exit options strengthen.
- Open protocols gain adoption.
- Platform visibility becomes auditable.

### Policy Implications

Access governance, antitrust, interoperability, transparency, public protocols, and digital rights become central.

### Open Questions

- How can access power be measured before it becomes irreversible?

## 6. Hybrid Reform Scenario

AI productivity gains are partially redistributed through taxes, sovereign AI equity, public AI infrastructure, wage-floor reform, and access-governance reforms.

### Core Mechanism

Society preserves AI acceleration while updating fiscal, ownership, labor, and access institutions to maintain circulation and agency.

### Current Signals Supporting It

- Current signals are mixed rather than conclusive.
- Existing policy tools can be adapted.
- AI gains may create enough surplus to fund transition.

### Early Warning Indicators

- Public AI infrastructure expands.
- Compute and platform transparency improve.
- AI dividends or equity mechanisms emerge.
- Wage-floor and labor-transition policies strengthen.

### Falsification Signals

- Reforms are captured or delayed.
- Concentration accelerates faster than governance.
- Public systems become dependent on private platforms.

### Policy Implications

Preserve AIRTF's tax architecture as active hypotheses while adding access governance, falsification metrics, and pathway-specific policy sequencing.

### Open Questions

- Which combination of tax, ownership, labor, and access reforms is most feasible before transition stress becomes severe?
