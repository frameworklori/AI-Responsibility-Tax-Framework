# UBI Survival Not Demand Solution

UBI can preserve survival liquidity, but it does not automatically create dynamic demand, ownership participation, or economic agency.

## Current Reality Signal

Governments may use tax credits, transfers, subsidies, public services, and deficit spending to stabilize households through transition stress.

These tools can stabilize short-term demand by keeping cash flow in the household sector. Government deficits and tax policy may already be acting as liquidity buffers in parts of the economy.

But transfers may not restore long-term aspiration, ownership access, productive participation, skill formation, or the ability to influence what society produces.

## Possible Development Path

- A. Effective transition bridge: subsidies help workers move into new productive roles.
- B. Subsidized survival equilibrium: transfers prevent collapse but do not restore agency.
- C. Fiscal dependency: household demand becomes increasingly dependent on state support.
- D. AI dividend plus participation economy: income support is paired with ownership, entrepreneurship, public AI infrastructure, and access rights.

## Indicators to Watch

- transfer payment dependency
- deficit-to-GDP ratio
- labor force participation
- entrepreneurship rates
- household formation
- consumer spending beyond essentials

## What Would Falsify This Risk

- subsidies successfully bridge workers into new productive roles
- AI dividends are paired with ownership and participation rights
- entrepreneurship and household formation improve
- dependency declines over time

## Open Questions

- What mix of income support, ownership, and access rights turns survival liquidity into participation?
- When does deficit-supported consumption become a bridge, and when does it become dependency?
