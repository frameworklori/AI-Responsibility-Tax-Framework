# Physically Necessary Labor and Wage Floor

AI may compress some cognitive labor while physical necessary labor remains socially essential but politically underpriced.

## Current Reality Signal

Service, care, logistics, food, construction, maintenance, and cleaning labor remain necessary. These jobs are often difficult to fully automate in variable real-world settings because they require movement, trust, physical presence, local judgment, and adaptation to messy environments.

Governments and firms often resist wage-floor increases because of inflation concerns, service costs, budget pressure, and business-model fragility.

Migrant labor may act as a wage-suppression valve. This can keep some services affordable while masking the social value of physically necessary work.

## AIRTF Structural Mapping

- Physically_Necessary_Labor_Undervaluation: tracks essential work that remains hard to automate but underpaid.
- Labor_Transition_Infrastructure: links transition policy to skills, mobility, and wage support.
- Responsibility_Tax_Model: offers a way to redirect AI-era surplus toward social stabilization.
- Demand circulation: essential workers have high marginal propensity to consume, so wage floors affect local circulation.

## Possible Development Path

- A. Wage-floor reform: essential labor wages rise with productivity support.
- B. Migrant-labor dependency: labor shortages are managed through low-wage inflows.
- C. Service inflation: wages rise but costs pass through to households.
- D. Robotics substitution: physical automation reduces some labor shortages.
- E. AI surplus used to support essential labor wages: productivity gains subsidize socially necessary work.

## Indicators to Watch

- minimum wage policy
- service-sector vacancies
- care labor shortages
- migrant labor dependency
- real wages in essential labor sectors
- robotics adoption in physical services

## What Would Falsify This Risk

- essential labor wages rise sustainably without major price shock
- automation genuinely relieves labor shortages
- productivity gains are shared across service sectors
- wage floors rise with household purchasing power

## Open Questions

- Which essential sectors can absorb wage increases without destabilizing affordability?
- Can AI surplus finance care, service, and maintenance work without creating dependency?
