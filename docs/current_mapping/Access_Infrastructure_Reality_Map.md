# Access Infrastructure Reality Map

The 20th-century debate focused on ownership of production. The AI-era debate must also track ownership of access.

## Current Reality Signal

AI assistants, search engines, recommendation systems, app stores, cloud platforms, payment rails, identity systems, and credit systems increasingly mediate discovery and participation.

Users may formally have market choice while real visibility and access are infrastructure-filtered. A firm may be legally free to sell, a worker may be legally free to offer services, and a creator may be legally free to publish, while ranking, payment access, assistant defaults, identity verification, app-store policy, or compute access determine practical reach.

## AIRTF Structural Mapping

- Integration_Layer_Monopoly: tracks cross-layer control over user-facing AI, data, distribution, and infrastructure.
- Compute_Feudalism: maps dependence on upstream compute owners.
- Digital_Chokepoint_Economics: tracks infrastructure layers that can extract rent or control participation.
- Regulatory_Capture_Risk: maps soft control through visibility, ranking, identity, payment, credit, and recommendation systems.

## Possible Development Path

- A. Competitive access ecosystem: users and firms can switch services and reach markets through multiple channels.
- B. Platform dependency: economic actors rely on a small set of platforms for visibility and transactions.
- C. Soft allocation economy: AI assistants and recommendation systems increasingly decide what people see or consider.
- D. Access feudalism: platform or state gateways control discovery, identity, payment, credit, compute, and assistant layers.
- E. Public/open access infrastructure reform: open protocols, public-interest infrastructure, and interoperability reduce dependency.

## Indicators to Watch

- search/recommendation market concentration
- AI assistant default distribution
- cloud/compute access concentration
- payment and identity chokepoints
- app-store gatekeeping
- small-business dependence on platform visibility

## What Would Falsify This Risk

- interoperability improves
- open protocols gain adoption
- exit options strengthen
- platform visibility becomes auditable
- SMEs gain access without dependency

## Open Questions

- Which access layers are most measurable with public data?
- How should regulators distinguish useful curation from hidden allocation power?
