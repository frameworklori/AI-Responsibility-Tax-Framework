# Stock Market vs Household Liquidity

Stock-market strength does not automatically mean broad economic circulation is healthy.

## Current Reality Signal

AI and mega-cap tech valuations remain strong. Asset markets can reflect expected productivity, monopoly rents, compute scarcity, data advantages, and platform power.

Household debt and credit stress remain important. U.S. household debt reached around $18.8 trillion in Q4 2025, with credit card and auto delinquencies elevated relative to the past decade.

Wealth effects are concentrated among asset holders. Rising equity values may support spending for higher-income households while doing less for households dependent on wages, credit, rent burden, and liquid cash flow.

## Possible Development Path

- A. Wealth-effect stabilization: rising markets support consumption enough to buffer weakness.
- B. K-shaped economy: asset owners gain while wage-dependent households face liquidity stress.
- C. Asset bubble correction: AI valuations adjust and expose fragile demand or debt conditions.
- D. Policy-supported liquidity bridge: fiscal policy, transfers, or tax changes stabilize household cash flow.

## Indicators to Watch

- S&P 500 concentration
- top 10% equity ownership
- household debt service
- credit card delinquency
- consumer spending by income group
- stock buybacks vs wage growth

## What Would Falsify This Risk

- broad household liquidity improves alongside stocks
- wage growth catches up
- consumer debt stress falls
- asset ownership broadens

## Open Questions

- How much of AI valuation reflects broad productivity versus infrastructure rent expectations?
- Can asset-market gains circulate broadly without deliberate ownership or tax mechanisms?
